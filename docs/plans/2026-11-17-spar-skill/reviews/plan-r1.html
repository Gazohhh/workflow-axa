Goals and non-goals

Goals

  • You can type `/spar <problem> [depth]` in both hosts. Only you can start it. The model never starts it by itself (R1, A1).
  • A fresh Claude and GPT write blind opening positions. Then they exchange objections for the chosen depth: quick 1, standard 3, deep 5, a number, or until-agreement. The default comes from your settings.json (R4, R5).
  • The debate ends with one named outcome and a result. The result names the recommended approach, its evidence, rejected alternatives, risks, foundation changes, unresolved objections, your decisions and who debated (R6, R7, step 7).
  • You are asked only what belongs to you: requirements, priorities, trade-offs or acceptable risk. Objective facts are researched instead (R8).
  • Inside a task, the result becomes the Approach part of the agreement, and /workflow continues with the HOW. Without a task, no files are written (R1, R10).
  • When GPT is not reachable, /spar tells you and asks how to proceed (R9).
  • Live runs show how a model follows the skill, in one real decision and in staged scenarios (A4, A5).

Non-goals

  • /spar does not plan the HOW. Files, interfaces, tests, the plan and the approval stay with workflow-specify (R1).
  • /spar never implements and never refactors, even when it finds a weak foundation. It shows the evidence and the alternatives instead (R1, R12).
  • Sparring never starts automatically. The workflow may recommend /spar for high-stakes work, but it never runs it (D3).
  • No separate SPAR.md. The result lives in the agreement or in the conversation (D4).
  • No third judge model, no code or experiments run during a debate, and no prototypes.
  • No change to T3 Code.
  • The fix for the approval hook bug (FINDINGS F1) is not part of this plan. You asked for it, and it becomes a separate task (D12).
  • No version bump and no CHANGELOG entry in this task. The release is a separate step.

The design

Overview

/spar is one small skill with one reference file. `skills/spar/SKILL.md` holds the steps. `skills/spar/T3.md` holds the T3 Code parts: the capability check, the debater brief, the ledger format and cancel. The skill owns only the debate. It uses grilling, workflow-clarify step 1, codebase-design and domain-modeling for everything else (R2).

The coordinator is the model you talk to. It first frames the decision in one sentence, with 3 to 6 criteria, the depth and the two debaters. Then it checks the premises that the decision depends on. Each premise becomes a verified fact, an unverified assumption or a contradiction (R3, R12). The amount of checking follows the risk of the decision, not the depth (R13).

You are the third participant. If something only you can decide blocks the frame or the debate, the coordinator asks you through grilling. Objective facts are researched and never asked (R8).

In the blind opening, a fresh Claude subagent and GPT get the same brief and write one position each. Neither sees the other. Each position has a recommendation, the rejected alternatives, the foundation weaknesses and the strongest uncertainty (R4).

In each exchange, both debaters get the latest position of the other side and the objection ledger. The ledger lists objections with stable IDs, each one open, resolved, needs you, or unverifiable. Each debater returns its strongest objection with evidence, its replies and what it changed. The coordinator argues no side. It checks the cited evidence itself, challenges agreement without evidence, and writes one progress line per round (R11).

The debate ends with one outcome, and the first match wins: cancelled, unavailable or interrupted, target changed, needs you, agreed, stalled, limit reached. Agreed needs both debaters to judge the same proposal version (R7). Stalled comes only after the coordinator found no question to you that could unblock the round (R6). Real disagreement is reported and never forced into agreement (R14).

The result lists the recommended approach, its evidence and the rejected alternatives. It also lists risks, required foundation changes, unresolved objections, your decisions, who debated, whether it was cross-model, and the outcome. Inside a task, the rounds go to FINDINGS, your choices go to DECISIONS, and the result becomes the Approach part of the agreement (D4). Then workflow-clarify asks you to agree, and workflow-specify plans the HOW. Without a task, the result stays in the conversation.

If GPT is missing or the T3 Code capability check fails, the coordinator says so and asks you how to proceed. One option you can choose is a fresh helper of the same model, labelled "single model, fresh context, not cross-model" (R9). You can stop the debate in plain words at any time, which also cancels running delegates (R6).

The main trade-off is cost against independence. Each exchange launches two fresh debaters, so the coordinator does not anchor the debate. This doubles the model runs per round (D5, FINDINGS S4 C1).
